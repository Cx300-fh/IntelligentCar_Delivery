From Problem To Answer

Real scene / Order

What if ordering is harder than the delivery itself?

Campus users may be walking, carrying items, or simply unwilling to operate a complicated console. The interaction has to disappear into the task.

Speak or tapChoose pickupPreview routeDispatch

Web Order UI

One real interface carries pickup, destination, route preview and live delivery feedback.

Voice interaction

Natural-language requests enter the same task pipeline through the PC voice gateway.

03

Order lifecycle

Validated requests become persistent pickup/drop-off stops before dispatch begins.

Real scene / Cargo

Reaching a place does not prove the package is there.

Pickup and handoff need physical evidence and clear interaction. Cargo state, user prompts and the compartment itself all become part of the delivery loop.

OpenLoadVerifyHandoff
01

Payload verification

Load sensing and the cargo bay provide physical evidence that an item was loaded or removed.

02

On-car handoff

Screen events, user confirmation and arrival handling close the pickup/drop-off loop on the vehicle.

Real scene / Move

The road changes. The payload changes. Motion has to adapt.

Real delivery is not one fixed controller on one clean route. The car has to perceive the road, localize itself, estimate cargo load, change turning behavior, avoid blocked edges and still execute safely.

PerceiveWeighLocalizeRe-planTuneControl
01

Perception & localization

Camera processing and AprilTag recognition anchor the car to physical campus nodes.

02

Payload measurement

Measured payload becomes a real control input instead of an assumed empty-car condition.

03

Turn Around

A dedicated multi-stage U-turn behavior handles routes that require the vehicle to reverse its travel direction.

04

Obstacle handling

Detected obstacles are converted into a physical stop or blocked-road condition before replanning.

05

Navigation & rerouting

The current node, destination, blocked edges and weighted route are exposed as one interactive campus graph.

06

Frame & shell

The enclosure protects electronics, stabilizes the cargo layout and turns the prototype into a usable product.

Real scene / Scale

What happens when several users need one robot?

A useful campus delivery system has to coordinate orders, capacity, stops and routes instead of treating every request as an isolated demo.

OrdersSchedulePlanDispatch
01

Dispatch scheduler

Multiple orders are merged into one feasible stop sequence with capacity and detour checks.

02

Weighted route planner

Distance, penalties and blocked edges determine the route that the PC recommends.

03

PC ↔ car gateway

NDJSON commands, heartbeats and vehicle events tie dispatch to physical execution.

Real scene / Trust

Autonomy also means knowing when not to move.

Network loss, stale commands, emergency intervention and restart recovery have to end in a predictable physical state, not just an error message.

DetectInhibitStopSyncResume
01

Fail-safe control

Safety inhibit, watchdog and PWM shutdown converge on the same predictable stopped state.

02

Link recovery & sync

Reconnect restores authoritative mission state before the vehicle is allowed to move again.

EVERYTHING TOGETHER

One closed loop from intent to physical delivery.

The final architecture only appears after the problems are understood: interaction creates the task, the PC coordinates it, and the vehicle turns commands into perception-aware motion and verified handoff.
